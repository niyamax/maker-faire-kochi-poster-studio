# Content Studio art direction

The content system must produce cultural-festival artwork with the confidence of an international design event. Information architecture is necessary; an administrative form is not a poster composition.

## Creative ownership

A creative director reviews the exported artwork, including real photographs and real event information, before a template is considered ready. Review the artwork outside the editor chrome. Functional completeness does not establish visual quality.

## Identity: making in progress

Maker Faire celebrates people constructing, adapting and sharing things. The existing responsive alphabet gives the studio a distinctive constructed voice. Use that voice, physical scale, purposeful image crops and a disciplined colour field to connect the series.

The colour foundation remains Maker Faire red, cyan, white and dark ink. Composition, image selection, typography and rhythm create variation. Dark ink on cyan provides a useful, readable pairing. Logo artwork retains its supplied proportions and selected colour version.

Reference principles:

- [Thonik: five years of Dutch Design Week campaigns](https://thonik.nl/stories/5-years-of-ddw-campaigns): a recognisable constructed alphabet can support an evolving identity across themes.
- [Pentagram: London Design Biennale](https://www.pentagram.com/work/london-design-biennale/story): the identity frames and supports exhibitors' work, with a clear graphic idea across applications.

These references set a standard for authored systems and cultural confidence. They are not layouts to reproduce.

## Page roles

### Cover: a reason to stop

One strong headline and one dominant image create the first reading. Use intentional asymmetry and scale. A photograph may meet an edge; it must show the project rather than become a tiny thumbnail. Maker or host information has a deliberate secondary position. A content label is optional and subordinate.

### Details: a reason to stay

Use an editorial composition. Readable text, purposeful crop and a meaningful relationship between them explain the project or workshop. Workshop facts sit directly on the colour field. Body text has a measured width and comfortable leading. The display alphabet should not be forced to carry every sentence.

### Closing: a clear invitation

The date, time, venue and action have a confident typographic hierarchy. Give the action and practical information visual weight. Do not place a miniature registration form under an enormous generic slogan. A selected photograph should contribute to the composition.

## Non-negotiable review criteria

- The first reading is clear at phone size and as a thumbnail.
- Every page has a visual idea beyond filling a template's rectangles.
- Cover, details and closing have distinct silhouettes while belonging to one series.
- Facts sit on the composition; white metadata cards and nested information panels are excluded.
- Typography uses controlled line lengths, scale and rhythm. Long content triggers editing guidance rather than microscopic type.
- Photographs have a deliberate focal point and enough scale to communicate their subject.
- Negative space has a compositional role. It is not a gap left after fixed boxes were stacked.
- A compact identity signature replaces the repeated spreadsheet-like footer. Main event facts are not duplicated as tiny footer copy.
- Graphic devices have meaning. Random polygons, fake diagrams, gratuitous arrows, checker patterns, shadows and gradients do not substitute for direction.
- Makers and their work stay central. The identity supports content and does not erase its individuality.
- Motion has the same hierarchy as the still. Functional information stays stable and readable.
- Review uses actual exported feed and Story artwork with photography, long real names, complete dates and venue, and the supported palette and logo variants.

## Review process

1. Inspect cover, details and closing artwork together as a series at phone size.
2. Inspect each page at full size for crop, spacing, text hierarchy and identity.
3. Compare feed and Story as composed adaptations of one idea.
4. Record concrete visual defects and fix them before approval.
5. Keep current proof images with the change for human review.

Automated assertions do not replace this creative review. Follow the user's current verification instructions for the project.

## Current creative review

The revised Maker Spotlight and Workshop artwork is approved for this release. Cover pages now pair responsive display lettering with a large image that meets the edge and an asymmetric maker/host rail. Details pages use a separate editorial silhouette with a tall image crop and readable text column. Closing pages place the date, time and invitation directly on the field at a deliberate scale, with practical details occupying the lower composition.

The review included real-photo feed and Story layouts, cyan invitations, the red and white field treatments, compact transparent identity marks, and the automatic contrast behavior used by newly created packs. White information panels, repeated event-fact footers and fake placeholder graphics have been removed. Authored title line breaks are preserved.
